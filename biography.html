Phase 2 · Explore & Explain Inductive Discovery Stations
📌 Visit each Station in order. Observe the examples, spot the pattern, then click Reveal My Rule to check your discovery and earn XP!
A
The Time, Place & Origin Sorter
Prepositions — on / in / to
Case 1
She was born on August 9, 1933.  ·  She was born on Monday.
Case 2
She was born in 1933.  ·  She lived in Tokyo.  ·  She was born in Japan.
Case 3
A child was born to Mr. and Mrs. Kuroyanagi.  ·  A gift was bought for her parents.
  • 1
    What preposition comes before a specific calendar date or day?
  • 2
    What preposition comes before a year, city, or country?
  • 3
    When naming whose child someone is, do we say born to parents or born for parents?
RULE 1: Use on before specific calendar dates and named days of the week.
RULE 2: Use in before years, major cities, and countries.
RULE 3: Use to (not for) when stating the parents to whom a child was born.
B
The Time-Machine Verb Lab
Tenses & Infinitives — Simple Past · Base Form
Case 1
Tetsuko completed her education in Tokyo. (past completed event)
Tetsuko completes her homework every evening. (present habit)
Case 2
She was born in 1933. ✔  ·  She is born today. ✗ (wrong for historical figures)
Case 3
The book went on to sell 8 million copies.  ·  She wanted to study music.
(Notice: We do NOT say "to sold" or "to studied".)
  • 1
    Which tense is used for historical biographical events that are already completed?
  • 2
    When a verb follows to in an infinitive chain (went on to…), does it change to past tense?
RULE 4: Past biographical milestones must be written in the Simple Past tense.
RULE 5: After the marker to in an infinitive chain, the verb must remain in its base form (e.g., to sell, not to sold).
C
The Sound & Identity Desk
Articles & Quantifiers — a / an / the / many
Case 1
Tetsuko is an actress. (/æ/ vowel sound)  ·  Tetsuko is an author. (/ɔː/ vowel sound)
Case 2
Tetsuko is a television personality. (/t/ consonant)  ·  Tetsuko is a writer. (/r/ consonant)
Case 3
She is the Goodwill Ambassador for UNICEF. (unique, official title)
She met a goodwill worker. (one of many)
Case 4
She received many awards.  ·  She received one award.
  • 1
    Why does actress take an while television personality takes a?
  • 2
    Why is Goodwill Ambassador for UNICEF preceded by the instead of a?
  • 3
    When the quantifier many is used, does the noun stay singular or become plural?
RULE 6: Use an before a vowel sound; use a before a consonant sound.
RULE 7: Use the definite article the when referring to a unique, official post or title.
RULE 8: The quantifier many must always be followed by a plural countable noun.
D
The Connector & Comparison Desk
Relative Pronouns & Superlatives — who / which · highest
Case 1
She thanked the doctor who treated the patients. (person)
She received the honour, which is the highest award in broadcasting. (thing/award)
Case 2
A high honour → A higher honour → The highest honour in Japan.
  • 1
    Which relative pronoun links back to a non-human thing, prize, or entity?
  • 2
    When the comes directly before a descriptive degree, which ending is required?
RULE 9: Use who to refer to people; use which to refer to things, awards, or non-human entities.
RULE 10: When preceded by the definite article the in an absolute comparison, use the superlative degree (e.g., the highest).

Rules 1–3: Prepositions

on → specific dates & days · in → years, cities, countries · to → born to parents

Rules 4–5: Tenses

Simple Past for completed biographical events. Base form after infinitive marker to.

Rules 6–8: Articles

an → vowel sound · a → consonant sound · the → unique official title · many + plural noun

"an actress · a writer · the Ambassador · many awards"
Rules 9–10: Connectors

who → persons · which → things/awards · the + superlative for absolute comparison
