A
When Do We Use On, In, and To?
Prepositions — on / in / to
  • 📅
    Sachin Tendulkar was born 24 April 1973.
    We use on when we say a specific date — like 24 April or a Monday.
  • 🏙️
    He was born Mumbai. He was born 1973.
    We use in when we say a city, a country, or just a year (without a date).
  • 👨‍👩‍👦
    A baby girl was born Mr. and Mrs. Sharma.
    We use to (not for) when we say who the parents are.
Case 1
She was born on August 9, 1933.  ·  She was born on Monday.
Case 2
She was born in 1933.  ·  She lived in Tokyo.  ·  She was born in Japan.
Case 3
A child was born to Mr. and Mrs. Kuroyanagi.  ·  A gift was bought for her parents.
  • 1
    Which small word do we use before a specific date or day of the week?
  • 2
    Which small word do we use before a year, a city, or a country?
  • 3
    When we say whose child someone is, do we use to or for?
RULE 1: Use on before a specific date or a day of the week. (on 15 October · on Monday)
RULE 2: Use in before a year, a city, or a country. (in 1931 · in Rameswaram · in India)
RULE 3: Use to (not for) when saying the parents to whom a child was born. (born to Mr. and Mrs. Kuroyanagi)
B
Talking About the Past — Which Verb Form?
Past Tense & Infinitives — was/completed · to + base form
  • 🏏
    Dhoni India to win the World Cup in 2011.
    We use a past tense verb (led, not leads) because this already happened in the past.
  • 🎓
    Aryabhata born in 476 AD. He mathematics.
    For famous people from history, we always use was/were and past tense verbs — never "is" or "studies".
  • 📚
    Her book went on to millions of copies. She wanted to music.
    After the word to, the action verb stays in its simple form — never "sold" or "studied".
Case 1
Tetsuko completed her education in Tokyo. (already done in the past)
Tetsuko completes her homework every evening. (present habit)
Case 2
She was born in 1933. ✔  ·  She is born today. ✗ (wrong for people from history)
Case 3
The book went on to sell 8 million copies.  ·  She wanted to study music.
(We do NOT say "to sold" or "to studied".)
  • 1
    When writing about someone's life from the past, which tense do we use — past or present?
  • 2
    When a verb comes right after the word to, does it change to past tense?
RULE 4: Use the Simple Past tense for things that already happened in a person's life. (completed, was, studied)
RULE 5: When the action word comes after to, keep it in its base (simple) form. (to sell — not to sold; to study — not to studied)
C
A, An, or The — Which One Fits?
Articles & Quantifiers — a / an / the / many
  • 🍎
    She ate apple. She ate banana.
    Say an before a word that starts with a vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u). Say a before a word that starts with a consonant sound.
  • 🏅
    Milkha Singh was runner. He received Padma Shri Award.
    Use a for one of many things. Use the for a unique, one-and-only title or award — there is only one Padma Shri!
  • 🏆
    India has won many cricket . India won one today.
    Use many with plural nouns (trophies, not trophy). For just one, use the singular form.
Case 1
Tetsuko is an actress. (starts with vowel sound 'a')  ·  Tetsuko is an author. (starts with vowel sound 'o')
Case 2
Tetsuko is a television personality. (starts with consonant 't')  ·  Tetsuko is a writer. (starts with consonant 'r')
Case 3
She is the Goodwill Ambassador for UNICEF. (one special, official title)
She met a goodwill worker. (one of many workers)
Case 4
She received many awards.  ·  She received one award.
  • 1
    Why does actress need an but television personality needs a?
  • 2
    Why is Goodwill Ambassador for UNICEF written with the and not a?
  • 3
    When you use the word many, should the noun be one thing or more than one?
RULE 6: Use an before words that start with a vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u). Use a before words that start with a consonant sound.
RULE 7: Use the for a special, one-and-only official title or post. (the Goodwill Ambassador — there is only one!)
RULE 8: The word many must always be followed by a plural noun (awards, not award).
D
Joining Sentences — Who or Which?
Joining Words & Comparisons — who / which / the highest
  • 👩‍⚕️
    She thanked the doctor helped her.
    Use who when you are joining a sentence about a person.
  • 🎖️
    She received an award, is the most famous prize in Japan.
    Use which when you are joining a sentence about a thing, such as an award, a book, or a prize.
  • 🥇
    It was a high honour → It was the honour in the country.
    When we say something is number one — the absolute best — we add -est or use most and put the in front.
Case 1
She thanked the doctor who treated the patients. (person)
She received the honour, which is the highest award in broadcasting. (thing/award)
Case 2
A high honour → A higher honour → The highest honour in Japan.
  • 1
    Which joining word — who or which — do we use for a thing like a prize or award?
  • 2
    When we say something is the number one, what ending do we add to the describing word?
RULE 9: Use who to refer to people. Use which to refer to things, prizes, or awards.
RULE 10: When something is the absolute best, use the with the superlative form (add -est or use most). Example: the highest, the greatest.
